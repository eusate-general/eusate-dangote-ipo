An initial public offering (IPO) is when a company sells shares to the public for the first time. The price per share is set in advance (the offer price), investors apply during the offer period, and after it closes the company allots shares to applicants. If the offer is oversubscribed (more demand than shares), applicants may receive fewer shares than they applied for, and the unused money is returned through the platform they applied on.

After allotment, the shares are listed on an exchange (for this offer, the Nigerian Exchange, NGX) and can then be bought and sold by anyone with a brokerage or CSCS account. The market price after listing can be higher or lower than the offer price. Nobody can promise a return.

A greenshoe (over-allotment option) is a provision that lets the issuer sell some extra shares if demand is higher than the base offer.

Things worth weighing before investing in any IPO: how much you can afford to lose, how long you can leave the money invested, whether you are diversified, and what you have read in the official prospectus, especially the risk section. A licensed stockbroker or financial adviser can help with personal decisions.
